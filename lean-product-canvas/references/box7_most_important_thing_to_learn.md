# Complete AI Prompts by Canvas Box
## Product Discovery for Agile Teams

This document organizes all AI prompts (Generation, Selection, and Review) by Lean Product Canvas box for easy reference.

---

## 🎯 Core Principle

**AI outputs are assumptions that must be validated, not facts to be accepted.**

Use these prompts to accelerate your discovery work, but always treat AI-generated content as assumptions requiring validation with real evidence.

---

## How to Use This Document

**Three types of prompts for each Canvas box:**

1. **GENERATION PROMPTS** (Optional)
   - Use when: You need ideas, are stuck, or want alternatives
   - AI does: Generates multiple options
   - You do: Evaluate and treat as assumptions

2. **SELECTION PROMPTS** (After Generation)
   - Use when: AI gave you too many options
   - AI does: Asks you questions about options
   - You do: Choose ONE focus + organize backlog

3. **REVIEW PROMPTS** (Recommended)
   - Use when: After you've completed work manually
   - AI does: Critiques format, clarity, identifies assumptions
   - You do: Revise your own work

---

# BOX 7: Most Important Thing to Learn

## Purpose

Identify the most important risk contained in your hypothesis - the assumption that, if wrong, would invalidate everything.

---

## GENERATION PROMPT: Most Important Thing to Learn

**When to use:** After writing hypothesis, need to identify riskiest assumption

```
I'm working on Box 7 (Most Important Thing to Learn) of the Lean Product Canvas.

Here's my hypothesis from Box 6:
[PASTE YOUR HYPOTHESIS]

Help me identify the most important thing to learn by:
1. Listing all the assumptions embedded in this hypothesis
2. For each assumption, categorizing it as: Value, Usability, Feasibility, or Viability
3. For each assumption, rating the risk if we're wrong (High/Medium/Low)
4. For each assumption, rating our current confidence level (High/Medium/Low)
5. Identifying which assumption is both: high risk if wrong AND low confidence
6. Suggesting how we could test each assumption

Remember: In early stages, focus on risks to VALUE rather than feasibility.

Based on this analysis, what's the ONE most important thing we need to learn first?
```

**Validation Required:** Ensure you're focused on the riskiest assumption, not just the easiest to test.

---

## REVIEW PROMPT: Most Important Thing to Learn

**When to use:** After you've identified what you need to learn from your hypothesis

```
I've completed Box 7 (Most Important Thing to Learn) of the Lean Product Canvas. 
Please review my work and provide constructive feedback.

Here is what I've identified as most important to learn:
[PASTE YOUR BOX 7 CONTENT]

Context from Box 6 - My hypothesis:
[PASTE YOUR HYPOTHESIS FROM BOX 6]

Remember: Box 7 should identify the most important RISK contained in the hypothesis - 
the assumption that, if wrong, would invalidate everything.

Please provide feedback on:

1. FORMAT ADHERENCE
   - Is this focused on ONE specific risk/assumption?
   - Is it phrased as something to learn/test (not something to build)?
   - Does it directly relate to the hypothesis in Box 6?

2. CLARITY
   - Is the assumption/risk clearly stated?
   - Is it specific enough to design an experiment around?
   - Would team members agree this is the key risk?
   - Is this truly about risk to VALUE (not just feasibility)?
   - Can we imagine what evidence would answer this question?

3. RISKY ASSUMPTIONS
   - Is this actually the RISKIEST assumption, or just the easiest to test?
   - Are there other assumptions that might be more critical?
   - What happens if this assumption is wrong?
   - Have we chosen value risk over feasibility risk (as recommended for early testing)?

Do NOT rewrite my learning statement. Instead, provide specific guidance on how 
I can improve it myself.
```
